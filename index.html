About the forbidden times

Avoid general voluntary prayer after you pray Fajr until the Sun rises clear of the horizon, around solar noon until the Sun declines, and after you pray Asr until sunset. The after-Fajr and after-Asr periods start when you perform the prayer.

The highlighted rows show the shorter solar periods from the hadith of ‘Uqbah ibn ‘Amir. Sunrise and sunset use a 4° low-Sun estimate. Midday starts eight minutes before solar noon as a precaution. The visible signs take priority.

Prayer signs and sources

True dawn is light spreading across the eastern horizon. Fasting begins at that same dawn. Isha begins when the red twilight fades. The models estimate when that happens. A solar angle describes the Sun’s position. The colour and spread of the light depend on local conditions.

The first third and halfway point use sunset to the following Fajr. An estimated Fajr affects both markers.

Fajr and Isha angles

Angles estimate the religious signs. A missing crossing tells you about the Sun’s position.

Isha

The night runs from sunset to the next Fajr. Ibn ‘Uthaymin treated halfway as the end. Ibn Baz used halfway for the ordinary period and distinguished a necessity period that lasts to Fajr.

When your angle has no crossing

An existing crossing stays in use. A missing crossing only tells you about the Sun’s position. The estimate cannot establish what the local sky looked like.

How the methods differ

One seventh and angle fraction use the local interval between sunset and sunrise.

Local relative uses annual local twilight fractions and five-minute steps at either end of the period. Last valid day carries forward the last crossing’s clock time.

Seasonal Tayba uses the Tayba research-based timetable for the missing prayer. Use Compare to select the full Tayba timetable. Crossings only shows unavailable twilight times with a dash.
